## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Too many consequential actions are exposed together.** Each alert shows five actions plus history, sometimes with a sixth conditional action; routine logging sits beside “Fechou” and “Perdeu.” This increases decision time and makes a consequential mis-tap more likely. Make “Falei com ele” the primary action, move infrequent actions under “Mais ações,” and add confirmation or undo for status changes. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`. Evidence: [CardAlerta.tsx](C:/Users/robso/Desktop/PROJETOS_BASE/checklead/web/src/components/CardAlerta.tsx).

2. **[P1] The dashboard delays its urgent work behind metrics.** Four metrics precede “Agir agora,” so the user must scan a performance summary before reaching the follow-up queue. Put the alert queue first with a compact count; move secondary metrics below it or collapse them on narrow screens. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`. Evidence: [RadarPage.tsx](C:/Users/robso/Desktop/PROJETOS_BASE/checklead/web/src/pages/RadarPage.tsx).

3. **[P2] Quick-action feedback is weak and detached from context.** The action flow reloads data after success, while failures fall back to a browser `alert`; neither gives a durable, lead-specific recovery path. Show inline success/error feedback and preserve the lead context; offer undo when a status change is reversible. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`. Evidence: [RadarPage.tsx](C:/Users/robso/Desktop/PROJETOS_BASE/checklead/web/src/pages/RadarPage.tsx).

4. **[P2] The new-lead form is dense on phones.** Fixed two-column field groups can squeeze names and contact details at narrow widths, while modal scrolling can push actions away. Stack fields below a narrow breakpoint and keep submit/cancel controls reachable. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`. Evidence: [ModalNovoLead.tsx](C:/Users/robso/Desktop/PROJETOS_BASE/checklead/web/src/components/ModalNovoLead.tsx).

5. **[P2] Low-contrast text weakens the login screen.** Browser overlays flag both the “Detector de vazamento de vendas” subtitle and “Acessar Radar” button text. Verify the actual text/background pairs against WCAG AA and darken the muted text or adjust the primary color without losing the restrained palette. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize`.

## Cognitive Load

**High: 5 of 8 checklist failures.** The metrics compete with the main task; hierarchy does not immediately privilege the urgent queue; alerts offer more than four simultaneous choices; multiple outcomes must be compared at once; and lower-frequency actions are not progressively disclosed. Chunking and grouping are generally good, and lead context is visible rather than requiring users to remember it.

## Emotional Journey

**Arrive:** “Agir agora” and the risk amount communicate urgency, but the metrics-first layout delays action. **Assess:** Reasons and contact details support confidence; the long action row adds hesitation. **Act:** Quick updates feel fast, but an accidental close/loss status is difficult to reverse. **Leave:** The empty state is reassuring, though “Tudo sob controle!” may overstate what “no alerts” guarantees.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex, power user:** The alert card has no visible batch path or keyboard accelerator, and the five-plus choices slow repeated triage; “Fechou” and “Perdeu” are easy to trigger beside routine actions.
- **Sam, keyboard/screen-reader user:** Lead-form labels are not programmatically associated with their inputs; browser-alert recovery is not contextual. The login overlay also flags low-contrast subtitle/button text.
- **Jordan, first-timer:** “X vazando” and “X decididos” do not explain the alert or metric, and there is no visible explanation of alert rules or next-step guidance.
